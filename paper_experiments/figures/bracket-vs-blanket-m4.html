Two Cost Models

Blanket PSTATE.DIT and function-level DIT, both against the same unhardened build. Blanket is a fixed per-process tax that does not care what the request does. Function-level placement pays per crypto boundary crossing. Which is cheaper is a property of the workload, not of the mitigation — and these two panels are the two ends of that.

Reading it

and what not to claim

Blanket's penalty is invariant

On WordPress it is +2.1% to +2.6% across a 600-fold change in crossings. It suppresses the data-dependent optimisations for the whole process whether or not a secret is present, so it does not care what the request does.

Function-level tracks crossings

0.0% at 110 crossings, +1.1% at 8,230, +8.4% at 65,600, in step with the crossings column and with nothing else. Two different cost models — and the crossover sits at 12,000–14,100 crossings per request.

No shipped configuration reaches it

The most crypto-dense pattern WordPress supports at stock settings is application-password REST auth, which re-verifies with phpass on every request, and it sits at 8,230. Getting to 65,600 takes raising phpass to 216 rounds — a change security guidance recommends.

Blanket adds no instructions

Arms A and C are the same binary, differing only in whether an injected constructor runs one msr DIT, #1 before main. Its cost has no instruction component, which is why an IPC reading and a cycle reading agree on it.
