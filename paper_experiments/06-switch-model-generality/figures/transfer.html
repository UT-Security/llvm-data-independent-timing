Switch Model Transfer

Every silicon number in this series is Apple M5, whose MSR DIT is cheap. Do the results survive a core where it is not? Only where the toggle rate is low — and the penalty tracks the committed toggle rate almost linearly.

At the low-toggle end the two models are indistinguishable — 0.03 to 0.66 points, inside the noise. The per-write cost of ~34 cycles is the same range as the 24.0 cycles measured directly on silicon in experiment 02, with gem5 inflating as it does throughout this series.

Why 02 is insensitive and 03 is not

Ask how often a write executes, not how many exist

Experiment 02 commits exactly 49 DIT writes per signature at every knob point — a toggle rate roughly 50× lower — and the switch model costs it only +0.38 to +1.54 points. Experiment 03 at chunk=64 commits 19,065 writes in 4.1M cycles: one per ~217 cycles.

That is the general rule, and it is the same predictor the callee-saved ABI work arrived at from a completely different direction. Static switch counts do not predict it; committed ones do, which is why those counters were added to the simulator.

Limits

What these numbers are not

32 KB ROIs, not the native 8 MB. The hardened arms are pathologically slow to simulate at full size — one run exceeded 15 minutes where the base arm took seconds. Worth recording as a property of simulating high toggle rates, but it means these regions are small.

Two workloads, two extremes. The middle of experiment 03's sweep was not run under both models.

gem5 magnitudes are inflated throughout, consistently ~3× against silicon elsewhere in this series. The ordering and the proportionality are the transferable parts, not the absolute percentages.

“Serialising” is gem5's model, not a specific shipping core. It is the conservative end; a real core may sit anywhere between.
