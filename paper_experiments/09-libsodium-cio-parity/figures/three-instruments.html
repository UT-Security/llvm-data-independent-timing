Three Instruments, One Ordering

The pass's overhead against baseline, by placement granularity from coarse to fine, on Apple M4, Apple M5, and gem5 under both MSR DIT models. Every instrument that serialises the switch tells the same story: coarser is cheaper, and finer costs more. The one that renames it is flat at 1.0×, whatever the placement.

Overhead by granularity, per benchmark

Each panel is one of CIO's six benchmarks. Left to right: blanket (mode set once, no switches), whole-function placement, region placement (the shipped default), and the pre-2026-08-24 defaults (finest). Hover or focus a column for all four values.

y: overhead vs baseline, log scale · 1× is the baseline itself · labels mark the M5 and gem5-renamed values at the finest placement

Why a log axis. The values run from 0.98× to 7.10×. On a linear axis every ≈1.0× point would collapse into the baseline, and those points are the result: blanket is free everywhere, and the renamed switch makes every placement free.
The M5 is the steepest and gem5 the shallowest because the switch itself costs 43.6, 33.4 and 19–37 cycles on the three, and gem5's baseline is 2–4× longer. Same ordering on all three; the magnitudes are per-core.
Function vs region on gem5 is inside the rig's resolution. Those two sit within a point or two of each other; the rig resolves cross-policy gaps of ~10 points, not one. The fine arm's gap is real on every instrument.
Layout is controlled, not ignored. Silicon carries a NOP-switch twin (arm Z: 1.077× on M5, 1.087× on M4 for aes-gcm encrypt); gem5 carries one twin per policy. These ratios are the raw arms; the twin-corrected placement ranking is in the write-up.
