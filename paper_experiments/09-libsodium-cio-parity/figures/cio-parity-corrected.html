Experiment 09 · libsodium, CIO parity · two hosts

Nothing to Recover

Run the closest prior work’s experiment on their library, with their seeds and their drivers, and the first question answers itself. Blanket PSTATE.DIT costs 1.00×–1.02× on all six benchmarks, on two different chips, and is below 1 on argon2id. There is no headroom — so every selective placement policy spends switches to recover something worth nothing, and loses by up to 5.3×.

Apple M5 · Mac17,2 Apple M4 · Mac16,10 kperf, root, 15 reps libsodium 1.0.21 · --disable-asm 2026-09-02

These numbers are a correction. The first publication understated them 4× to 15×.

Each sample was timed between two kpc_get_thread_counters() calls — calls into the kperf driver, not register reads, with the counter sampled part-way through, so their cost lands inside the timed region. Measured directly against a payload of known length: 2,521 cycles on M5 and 3,234 on M4. That is 68% of the chacha baseline and 88% of the AES one. It cancels in arm-vs-arm differences, so the switch decomposition always held; it sits in the denominator of every ratio, which did not.

Re-timed with a 21-cycle counter on both machines, with a NOP-switch control added. All three conclusions survive. Only the magnitudes changed, and they changed in the direction that makes this experiment’s argument stronger.

Instrumenting a 55-tick region costs something through code movement alone, and an A-vs-P comparison books it as DIT cost. That is what arm Z is for.

Against CIO

same library, same seeds

CIO could not use the hardware mode and says so — at the time only Apple M-series shipped PSTATE.DIT, and Intel’s DOIT needed the kernel. Their overhead is the price of doing in software what the mode does in hardware.

The secrets really are protected

gem5 shadow taint

Static coverage says 108 functions instrumented and 0 severe information-loss records. It cannot say whether a secret ever reaches a covered instruction with the mode clear, because nothing faults when it does.

 The unhardened control is what makes the zero mean something: the same library without hardening runs every one of those instructions unprotected, so the oracle demonstrably reached the crypto.

And the oracle’s own propagation is verified, because a model that lost the key mid-algorithm would report the same clean zero. Seeding a public buffer of the same size at the same point collapses the count to 0; seeding nothing gives 0; the count scales exactly linearly with work (3,585 / 7,170 / 14,340 over 1, 2 and 4 rounds); and at 40.7% of all committed instructions the taint covers the whole primitive rather than a truncated prefix of it.

What this does not say

limits, recorded
  • It is not evidence against selective placement. It says where placement does not belong, and that you can know before writing any compiler. In experiment 02 the same library is the secret lane of a flow with a public lane and the pass beats blanket by 21%. The library is not the workload; the flow is.
  • CIO’s seeds over-taint. Their config marks all five arguments of crypto_sign, including the message length and the output buffer, which inflates the 126 static UNCOVERED sites — a branch on a length reported as a secret branch.
  • The oracle covers two of six paths. argon2id and AES-GCM are unaudited, and it is two operations per workload against the libsecp256k1 run’s 200. Misc registers are untracked by design, and the address channel is counted but never flagged, since DIT does not cover cache timing.
  • Three earlier counter claims were retracted: that blanket raises IPC (a whole-process artifact — the timed region is ±2%), that timed-region IPC was 12–14 (impossible on an 8-wide core; an instrumentation asymmetry, since fixed), and that DIT removes value-predictor flushes (flush counts move −0.1% to −7.3%).
