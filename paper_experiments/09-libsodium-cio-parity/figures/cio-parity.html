Experiment 09 · libsodium, CIO parity

Nothing to Recover

Run the closest prior work’s experiment on their library, with their seeds and their drivers, and the first question answers itself. Blanket PSTATE.DIT costs −0.60% to +1.95% on all six benchmarks. There is no headroom — so every selective placement policy spends switches to recover something worth nothing, and loses by 12% to 124%.

No dwell term

Blanket adds 0 ± 10 instructions and 0 ± 60 cycles per operation, measured the same way. The cost is switch serialisation alone — which is what “blanket is free” implies.

Coarser wins, everywhere

Whole-function placement (F) beats region placement (P) on all six. With dwell at zero, narrowing coverage buys nothing and you only pay toggles, so fewer toggles is strictly better.

Against CIO

same library, same seeds

CIO could not use the hardware mode and says so — at the time, only Apple M-series shipped PSTATE.DIT, and Intel’s DOIT needed the kernel. Their overhead is the price of doing in software what the mode does in hardware.

What this does not say

limits, recorded
  • It is not evidence against selective placement. It is the measurement that says where placement does not belong, and that you can know it before writing any compiler. In experiment 02 the same library is the secret lane of a flow with a public lane, and the pass beats blanket by 21%. The library is not the workload; the flow is.
  • CIO’s seeds over-taint. Their config marks all five arguments of crypto_sign, including the message length and the output buffer. That inflates the 126 UNCOVERED sites — a branch on a length is reported as a secret branch. The 49 AES-GCM secret-address sites are genuine; the total is not 126 real leaks.
  • Their AEAD drivers discard the additional data. additional_data_sz = 0; //strtol(...) — the Makefile passes AD = 100 and the driver ignores it. Reproduced exactly; the label is wrong in their Makefile, not in the measurement.
  • No dynamic verification. Coverage here is static. Whether every secret-dependent instruction actually executes with DIT on needs the gem5 shadow-taint oracle, which has not been run on this library.

Three counter claims were made and retracted while building this.

That blanket raises IPC by up to 9.4% — a whole-process artifact; the timed region is ±2%, and the two disagreed in sign on 5 of 6 benchmarks. That timed-region IPC was 12–14 — impossible on an 8-wide core, caused by reading the two counters in separate calls so the instruction window bracketed work the cycle window did not. That DIT removes value-predictor flushes — flush counts move −0.1% to −7.3%.

The switch-count decomposition above replaced all three, and only surfaced because one of the numbers was absurd enough to force a re-derivation.
