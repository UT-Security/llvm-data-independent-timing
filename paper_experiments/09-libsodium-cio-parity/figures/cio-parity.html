Experiment 09 · libsodium, CIO parity

Nothing to Recover

Run the closest prior work’s experiment on their library, with their seeds and their drivers, and the first question answers itself. Blanket PSTATE.DIT costs −0.60% to +2.07% on all six benchmarks, on two different chips. There is no headroom — so every selective placement policy spends switches to recover something worth nothing, and loses by 9% to 404%.

Apple M5 · Mac17,2 · 2026-09-01 Apple M4 · Mac16,10 · 2026-09-02 kperf, root 15 reps · median libsodium 1.0.21 · --disable-asm

The percentages first published here were understated by 4× to 15×.

Each sample was timed by the two kpc_get_thread_counters() calls bracketing the measured call — and those are calls into the kperf driver, not register reads. Their cost lands inside the timed region: 3,234 cycles, measured directly by fitting region cost against a payload of known length. That is 72% of the chacha baseline and 87% of the AES one. It cancels in the arm-vs-arm differences, so the switch decomposition below always held; it sits in the denominator of every percentage, which did not.

Re-timed with a 21-cycle counter, on a second chip, with a NOP-switch control added. All three conclusions survive — blanket is free, coarser beats finer, argon2id is free in every arm. Only their magnitudes changed, and they changed in the direction that makes this experiment’s argument stronger, not weaker.

What the denominator was doing

same chip, same binaries, one changed timer

A percentage is a ratio, so it never needed cycles at all — and the counter read that produced them cost more than the crypto it was measuring. Re-timed with CNTVCT_EL0, two instructions and a 21-cycle offset instead of 3,234. Nothing else changed: same host, same archives, same drivers, same reps.

Why this is a denominator fix

Extra cycles per op is a difference, so the offset has to cancel and the timer swap has to leave it alone. It does: ed25519 3,141 → 3,263, chacha encrypt 2,720 → 2,694, decrypt 2,979 → 2,960. The numerators held; the ratios moved.

It cuts the right way

This experiment argues that where the secret fraction reaches 100% there is nothing for placement to recover. Fixing the denominator makes selective placement look worse on four of six rows — the correction strengthens the claim it corrects.

No dwell term

Blanket adds 0 ± 10 instructions and 0 ± 60 cycles per operation, measured the same way. The cost is switch serialisation alone — which is what “blanket is free” implies.

Coarser wins, everywhere

Whole-function placement (F) beats region placement (P) on all six, on both chips. With dwell at zero, narrowing coverage buys nothing and you only pay toggles, so fewer toggles is strictly better.

Switches, or just moving the code?

Arm A differs from P by 521 instructions and therefore by every address after the first switch, so A-vs-P confounds the mode switching with the code movement. Arm Z has P’s exact instruction count and addresses — byte-identical but for 521 opcodes — so the two terms separate.

Against CIO

same library, same seeds

CIO could not use the hardware mode and says so — at the time, only Apple M-series shipped PSTATE.DIT, and Intel’s DOIT needed the kernel. Their overhead is the price of doing in software what the mode does in hardware.

Gate 1 had to be restated, and the old form could never have passed here.

It asserted a ratio, >3.5×, fitted to M5. But the chase starts at one cycle per hop and ends at L1 load-to-use, so the ratio’s ceiling is the L1 latency in cycles — four on M5, three on M4. A >3.5× test is therefore unsatisfiable on a three-cycle-L1 core no matter how well the mode works, and it duly printed FAIL — instrument cannot see DIT on a run where DIT plainly worked. The gate now asserts cycles per hop, which is the portable form. Compare it across chips that way, never as picoseconds and never as a ratio.

What this does not say

limits, recorded
  • It is not evidence against selective placement. It is the measurement that says where placement does not belong, and that you can know it before writing any compiler. In experiment 02 the same library is the secret lane of a flow with a public lane, and the pass beats blanket by 21%. The library is not the workload; the flow is.
  • CIO’s seeds over-taint. Their config marks all five arguments of crypto_sign, including the message length and the output buffer. That inflates the 126 UNCOVERED sites — a branch on a length is reported as a secret branch. The 49 AES-GCM secret-address sites are genuine; the total is not 126 real leaks.
  • Their AEAD drivers discard the additional data. additional_data_sz = 0; //strtol(...) — the Makefile passes AD = 100 and the driver ignores it. Reproduced exactly; the label is wrong in their Makefile, not in the measurement.
  • Two pieces of this rig are reconstructions, not the originals. The gate instrument and the indirect-call-resolution recipe behind arm N were never committed and the M5 machine’s copies are gone. Arm N is rebuilt to hit the recorded statics exactly — 749 switches, 164 functions, 250,136 bytes, 16 information-loss records — but matching numbers is the evidence, not matching method. Rebuilding the gate the obvious way, as a strided chase, returns a flat 1.00× that reads like a hardware finding and is really a broken instrument: the predictor is a constant-value predictor, and only a self-referencing node is predicted.
  • Dynamic verification is now done, on two of the six paths. The gem5 shadow-taint oracle reports zero under-taints in both hardened arms — 294,164 secret operations in ed25519 signing and 7,170 in chacha20-poly1305, every one of them executing with the mode set, against an unhardened control where all of them run clear. The argon2id and AES-GCM paths are still unaudited, and it is two operations per workload, not the 200 of the libsecp256k1 run.

Three counter claims were made and retracted while building this.

That blanket raises IPC by up to 9.4% — a whole-process artifact; the timed region is ±2%, and the two disagreed in sign on 5 of 6 benchmarks. That timed-region IPC was 12–14 — impossible on an 8-wide core, caused by reading the two counters in separate calls so the instruction window bracketed work the cycle window did not. That DIT removes value-predictor flushes — flush counts move −0.1% to −7.3%.

The switch-count decomposition above replaced all three, and only surfaced because one of the numbers was absurd enough to force a re-derivation.
