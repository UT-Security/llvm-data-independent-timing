kperf per-region instrumentation offset, measured on Apple M4 (Mac16,10)
========================================================================
2026-09-02, root, utils/cio_offset_probe.c, built with the same flags as
taint_libsodium_sudo_run.sh part 2 and going through eval_util.h's timer macros
so it measures the instrument the rig actually used.

WHAT THIS IS. CIO's drivers time one crypto call as
    start = START_CYCLE_TIMER; op(); end = STOP_CYCLE_TIMER;
and under root those macros are kpc_get_thread_counters() -- a call into the
kperf driver, not a register read, with the counter sampled part-way through. So
the tail of the opening call and the head of the closing one land BETWEEN the
two timestamps and are charged to the crypto. Every sample carries that as a
fixed additive offset.

It cancels in arm-vs-arm DIFFERENCES (extra cycles/op, cycles per switch) but
NOT in RATIOS, where it sits in the denominator -- and on the fast primitives it
IS most of the denominator. Consequences are recorded in ../README.md.

METHOD. Region cost against a payload of known cycle cost (a dependent ADD
chain, one retire per cycle). Sweeping the payload gives
    measured = slope * payload + intercept
The intercept is the offset. The slope must be ~1.0 under kperf, and is the
check that makes the intercept mean anything: it says the instrument ADDS to the
payload rather than perturbing it, so a single subtractable offset exists.

LIMIT. The payload is register-only, so slope ~1.0 shows the syscall does not
perturb REGISTER work. It says nothing about cache, TLB or branch-predictor
state, all of which a real crypto call depends on. If the syscall evicts lines
the crypto then refills, that cost is NOT in the intercept. So
(measured - intercept) is an UPPER bound on the uninstrumented cost and a direct
CNTVCT_EL0 measurement is a lower one. Extending the sweep with a cache-resident
payload would close this and has not been done.

The 2706 MHz reading is why run 2 exists. The rig's own ditprobe gate read
4406-4415 MHz on all 150 reps, i.e. P-cores, and a syscall costs a different
NUMBER OF CYCLES on an E-core. One clock sample taken after the sweep cannot say
which cluster the sweep sat on, so the probe was changed to sample the clock next
to every point and discard any pass that runs below full clock.

A LOW CLOCK IS NOT NECESSARILY AN E-CORE, and the "E !!" labels above are wrong.
Passes 1 and 2 ramp 1798 -> 3090 and 3143 -> 4105 MHz ACROSS the sweep: that is a
P-core coming up under DVFS from idle, not cluster migration. The probe cannot
tell the two apart, so it now says "low clock" and discards without claiming
which (utils/cio_offset_probe.c was corrected after this run; the labels here are
the output as captured).

Discarding those passes was right anyway, but note the offset barely depends on
it: every pass, ramping or not, landed in 3229-3306 cycles. That insensitivity is
a result, not an assumption -- which is why the discard stays in the tool.

The earlier indirect estimate, from differencing kperf cycles against wall-clock
CNTVCT for four benchmarks, was 3333/3359/3455/3517 -- about 5% high, because
wall-clock includes preemption where kperf counts thread cycles only. Use 3234.
