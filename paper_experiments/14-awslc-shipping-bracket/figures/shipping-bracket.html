The Shipping Bracket

AWS-LC ships the developer's DIT bracket as a build option and has never published what it costs. This is what it costs, on the vendor's own benchmark, one hard-pinned core, cycles counted by the hardware.

One bracket entry costs the same at every message size

AEAD-AES-128-GCM seal enters exactly one bracketed function per call. Cycles per operation for the six arms, against message size. The gap between AWS default and unhardened does not move as the message grows from 16 bytes to 16 KB: it is the price of the two mode-changing writes, and nothing else.

The three prices

From the two rows that enter one bracketed function per operation. A write that changes PSTATE.DIT serialises the core; a write that leaves it unchanged nearly does not; the barrier costs almost nothing right after a serialising write and a great deal after a non-serialising one.

Nesting multiplies it

Dividing each row's AWS default − unhardened by the price of one entry gives the number of bracketed entries an operation pays for. Two unit prices apply: an entry into an AEAD-level function (154 cycles on the seal row) and an entry into the single-block AES call (94 cycles; less is in flight to drain). The EVP cipher layer pays for several AEAD-level entries per call. CMAC calls the bracketed single-block AES once per 16 bytes and pays for 1,024 block entries per 16 KB.

The vendor's claim

Where coarse DIT changed the speed

Coarse DIT costs nothing on the crypto. On several rows it is faster than unhardened, and the AWS hoist arm, which also holds DIT on throughout, moves with it. The rows share one feature: fresh random bytes. On this core DIT disables the data-dependent prefetcher, and random words look like pointers to it. A hypothesis, stated as one.

The paper's ten rows

The set that carries the argument: the same 156-cycle entry at three message sizes (rows 3, 6, 7), the two faces of nesting (2 and 8), the barrier's price (4 against 3, and every + sb column against its base), a non-AES primitive (5), a long operation where nothing matters (9), and the row where coarse wins outright (10). Instructions and IPC, every arm

Cycles = instructions / IPC. The bracket adds a few instructions per entry; the cycles it adds come from the IPC it destroys.

Method

  • AWS-LC v5.8.0, built three ways with the platform compiler; tool/speed.cc patched to read PMC0 (cycles) and PMC1 (instructions retired), isb-ordered, around its own timed loop, and print both per JSON row. One thread, hard-bound to a P-core through kern.sched_thread_bind_cpu; the constructor reports the bind at exit and the driver checks it.
  • Each row: the operation is called in a loop for a fixed window; cycles and instructions are the window totals divided by the call count. Arms rotate on every rep; the first rep is warm-up; medians over the rest.
  • Nothing is dropped. Samples whose implied clock (cycles over microseconds) falls outside the P-core band are flagged and counted, never excluded; the count is in the status strip.
