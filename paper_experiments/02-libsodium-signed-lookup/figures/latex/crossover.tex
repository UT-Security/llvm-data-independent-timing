  \begin{subfigure}[t]{0.49\textwidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics{figures/crossover-panel-a-m4}
    \caption{Apple M4, shipping silicon. $f^* = 29.3\%$.}
    \label{fig:xover-m4}
  \end{subfigure}\hfill
  \begin{subfigure}[t]{0.49\textwidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics{figures/crossover-panel-b-gem5}
    \caption{gem5 Neoverse-V2 FDP. $f^* = 60.0\%$.}
    \label{fig:xover-gem5}
  \end{subfigure}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Where fine placement stops paying, on one microbenchmark and two
    machines.} The workload is a public lookup lane of $L$ dependent loads then
    one AES-256-GCM call over 64 secret bytes, repeated; sweeping $L$ sweeps the
    secret fraction $f$ on the $x$ axis. \emph{$f$ is a ratio of cycles}, measured on
    the unhardened build: $f = (c - c_\mathrm{pub})/c$, where $c$ is the
    workload's cycles and $c_\mathrm{pub}$ is the same binary with the crypto
    call skipped. Every iteration is identical, so the secret share of one and
    of the whole run are the same number. It is therefore a property of the workload rather than of any
    placement, so all three arms move along one fixed axis --- and it is why the
    same $L$ sits at a different $f$ on the two machines, gem5 sustaining
    IPC~1.07 at $L=10$ where the M4 sustains 3.89.
    \textbf{Coarse} holds \texttt{PSTATE.DIT} over
    the whole workload, so it costs the public lane its load-value predictions
    and gets dearer as $f$ falls. \textbf{Fine} brackets the mode around each
    crypto call instead --- read the previous DIT state, set it, speculation
    barrier, call, restore --- paying a fixed bill per call, so it gets cheaper.
    $f^*$ is where the two cross, and it is the deployment decision.
    \textbf{ExpeDITe} is that same fine placement under a renamed mode switch,
    and it is flat: 10--29 cycles a call, never crossing Coarse anywhere in the
    sweep. It is a microarchitectural design gem5 models and no shipping part
    implements, which is why it appears in (b) alone. $y$ is IPC overhead
    against an unhardened build, each fine arm measured against an
    instruction-matched NOP twin.
    %
    \emph{The two $y$ ranges differ, $+105\%$ against $+35\%$, and that is a
    result rather than a plotting choice}: one bracket costs 333--710\,cycles
    per iteration on the M4 against 206--351 in the model, over an iteration
    3.6$\times$ shorter, so it is 106\% of a secret-heavy iteration on hardware
    and 18\% of one in simulation. The simulator is optimistic about fine
    placement by roughly a factor of two in secret fraction.%
  }
  \label{fig:xover}
\end{figure*}
