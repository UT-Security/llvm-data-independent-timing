Which of these touch a secret:

  Symfony's suite request: NONE. Five hash() calls, no key, no HMAC, no crypt, no
  password function. Symfony hashes cache keys and class-map digests -- public data.

  WordPress's suite request: 4, all hash_hmac keyed with the site's salts.
  wp_hash($data,$scheme) is hash_hmac('md5',$data,wp_salt($scheme)) (pluggable.php),
  and wpdb::placeholder_escape() computes hash_hmac('sha256', uniqid(AUTH_SALT,true),
  AUTH_SALT) once per request (class-wpdb.php). These are long-lived SITE secrets,
  not a user credential. The other 106 calls are md5 over cache keys, transient names
  and gravatar hashes: public.

  Logged-in adds +5 hash_hmac and +1 hash_equals over anonymous -- the two cookie
  validations. The login POST adds ~8,100 md5 -- phpass at 2^13 rounds.

The Symfony login POST is the shape that matters most: THREE crossings (password_verify,
hash_equals, hash) for 1.62 billion cycles of key derivation, because bcrypt's whole
iteration loop lives inside one C call. WordPress's login is 8,193 crossings for 3.3 million
cycles of md5, because phpass's loop is PHP bytecode. Same security function, six orders of
magnitude apart in secret work per crossing -- and that, not the round count, is why one
costs nothing and the other costs 1.36 points.

Against a measured crossover of ~12,000 calls per request (bracket) and ~7,000 (pass),
the suite's own requests sit two to three orders of magnitude below the point where
blanket becomes the cheaper option.
