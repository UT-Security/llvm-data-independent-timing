Two Analyses, One Library

Every other experiment here picks its own seeds, which makes “you chose the annotations that flatter your pass” a fair objection that no internal measurement can answer. CryptoMPK's artifact ships an independent group's derived taint set. Run our pass on their exact source and compare.

Their build is x86‑64 -O0 LLVM IR; ours is aarch64 -O2 post-register-allocation MIR, so line-level agreement is noisy for reasons unrelated to either being wrong. The enclosing function is the stable unit.

Why libhydrogen, and only libhydrogen. Of their seven targets it is the one where the whole library is a single translation unit, so our per-TU analysis and their whole-program LTO analysis see the same code. On libsodium the eight relevant TUs mean our cross-TU limit would dominate and the number would measure that instead of precision.

The finding

The seed point decides, not the seed count

Both annotations say “the signing key is secret”. One names the sk argument; the other names the keypair buffer, which is the form CryptoMPK's own #pragma tainter taint(&key_pair) takes.

The argument seed is the one a developer writes first, and it leaves 88% of the seeded function and the entire scalar multiply unmarked.

Why. hydro_sign_prehash derives the ephemeral secret by hashing the long-term key - hydro_hash_init(&st, zero, sk), then hydro_hash_final(&st, eph_sk, ...). That last call writes a secret through an argument pointer, and our P0 mod-set summary carries no argument provenance, so the caller never learns eph_sk became secret.

Confirmed by repair. One line:

hydro_hash_final,1,pointee

takes hydro_sign_final_create from need=27 / coverage 12.0% to need=265 / 99.2%, pulls hydro_x25519_scalarmult (need=43) and hydro_x25519_core (need=170) into the analysis, and the library from 27 to 304 switches.

Two of them are the X25519 field and scalar multiplies - the arithmetic that actually carries the secret. Taint reaches hydro_x25519_core (need=170) and stops at its call into hydro_x25519_mul. Both are in-TU direct calls, so this is not the cross-TU limit; the suspected mechanism is pointee taint through frame-address arguments.

The whole premise of experiment 07 is that the compiler tells you where it lost the secret. Under the seed a developer would actually write, it does not.

The report's severity criterion is consequence at a call boundary - cross-TU, tail call, indirect. A secret lost to an imprecise mod-set inside one TU crosses no boundary it watches, so nothing fires. It should: the repair line above is exactly the kind the report already knows how to print.

Limits

What this is not

Static evidence only for the seven. That they are unmarked and not AlwaysEnteredWithDIT is our pass's own accounting; that they are secret-dependent is CryptoMPK's finding plus the source. Confirming they execute with DIT off needs the gem5 shadow-taint oracle, not run here.

Their ground truth is imperfect too. Their mxor declassification is unsound at object granularity. Read 79% as agreement between two imperfect analyses, never as a score against truth.

The “ours only” set is not simply over-taint. Outside driver scope it is dominated by 17 hydro_kx_* and 8 _hydro_pwhash_* functions their drivers never call, reached by the module-wide secret-global rule on the RNG state. That rule is also the only route by which any seed reaches the ephemeral signing key, which is fresh randomness.

No timing. Experiment 04 already measured blanket DIT as free on libhydrogen, so there is no cost question to ask of this workload.
